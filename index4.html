Founding story

A girl from Malindi who chose to act.

I grew up in Malindi, a coastal town in Kilifi County, Kenya — where the ocean is beautiful and opportunity is scarce, especially if you are a girl. Long before I understood words like "gender inequality" or "systemic barriers," I understood what it looked like to watch a girl vanish from your life.

My elder sisters were the first to go. One by one, each was pulled out of school and into early marriage before she could finish. I was young enough that I did not fully understand what had happened — only that they were there, and then they were not. Their futures had been decided for them, quietly, without their consent.

Then, when I was in Form 2, it happened to my best friend. I watched her leave school against her will, and something shifted in me permanently. This was not bad luck. This was a pattern — one that repeated itself across my community, year after year, girl after girl.

"What is the difference between me and the girls who had to leave? The honest answer was a scholarship — and the courage of one person who believed I was worth investing in."

In 2018, I received a scholarship to attend Matuga Girls High School. That opportunity did not just change my future — it showed me, in stark relief, how fragile a girl's education is in communities like mine. Without that scholarship, I would likely have faced the same fate as my sisters. I carry that knowledge everywhere I go.

Even at Matuga, the dropouts continued. Girls I had come to know would disappear mid-term — pulled away by pregnancy, poverty, or pressure to marry. Each time, I felt the urgency deepen. The problem was not a lack of ambition among these girls. It was the absence of systems designed to protect them.

That question — how can technology and education protect girls and give them a second chance? — became the foundation of everything I have built since.

In 2021, I channeled my lived experience into Girls I-Save Africa — Gisave. I designed it as a tech-enabled platform with three core functions: a safe, confidential system for reporting gender-based violence; direct connections to counselors and support networks; and pathways into STEM mentorship and opportunity. The premise was simple: if the systems that are supposed to protect girls do not reach them, we build new ones that do.

The scale of the problem

What I witnessed in Malindi is not unique.

The data confirms what I saw with my own eyes. Across Africa and the world, millions of girls are being pushed out of education by systems that were never built with them in mind.

132M
Girls out of school globally
Source: UNESCO, 2023
12M
Girls married before age 18 every year
Source: UNICEF
40%
Of girls in sub-Saharan Africa do not complete secondary school
Source: World Bank

In Kenya's coastal and arid counties — including Kilifi, Kwale, Tana River, and Wajir — these numbers are even starker. Early marriage, gender-based violence, and teenage pregnancy are the leading reasons girls leave school. The gap is not a lack of willing girls. It is a lack of infrastructure.

2021 · Young Scientists Kenya

Overall Winner — Social & Behavioral Sciences

Competing at the YSK National Science and Technology Exhibition, Gheida won the overall prize in the Social and Behavioral Sciences category for her project Girls I-Save — a tech platform to report GBV safely, connect girls to counselors, and open pathways into STEM. Representing Matuga Girls High School, Kwale County, she became one of the youngest innovators to win at this national level.

This prestigious award recognizes exceptional talent, innovation, and research excellence. The overall winner's research contributes to advancing knowledge in behavioral sciences, providing insights that inform policies and interventions aimed at improving individual and societal outcomes.

"Winning Young Scientists Kenya told me that a girl from Malindi could stand on any national stage and win. It was the moment I stopped seeing Gisave as a project and started seeing it as a movement."

The vision ahead

We are not building charity.
We are building infrastructure.

Gisave was born from grief — from watching people I loved lose their futures to systems that were never designed with them in mind. But it is being built with conviction.

Our vision is to build a fully functional digital platform accessible across Africa, strengthen partnerships with schools, governments, and global organizations, and expand mentorship and support systems to reach millions of girls.

Every girl deserves the tools to stay in school, stay safe, and imagine a future she chose for herself.

"I am building the platform I wish had existed when my best friend needed it most."
— Gheida Abdala Omar
